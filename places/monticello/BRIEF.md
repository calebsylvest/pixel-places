# Brief: Monticello

Status: first pass built.

```
Place:            Monticello, Thomas Jefferson's house near Charlottesville, Virginia, as completed in 1809.
Reference:        HABS VA-241 measured drawings (1989–92), Library of Congress, public domain;
                  sheets 1–12 are in references/. Sheet 3 = first floor plan (east at the top, north at
                  the left); sheet 1 = west elevation. Scale traced from the 109'-5" overall dimension:
                  about 0.0523 m per sheet pixel (1024 px wide JPEGs).
                  Monticello.org house FAQ: 110' × 87'9", 44'7" to the oculus; dome 31' × 28' (octagonal);
                  parlor 27'3" × 23'8", ceiling 18'2"; 43 rooms in all, 11 on the first floor.
Fixed by source:  first-floor plan: entrance hall and parlor on the east–west axis; north side: north
                  octagon, north square, piazza, north passage, tea room, dining room; south side:
                  south square, library and book room, south passage, Jefferson's bedroom with its
                  alcove bed, cabinet (study), greenhouse; four corner porches. Porticoes east (entrance)
                  and west (garden), four columns each. Octagonal dome over the west front. L-shaped
                  terraces over the service wings, ending in pavilions.
Interpretations:  wall heights and window positions are simplified; doors placed by eye from the plan;
                  terrace wings 20 m north/south then 45 m west, built on flat ground (the real site
                  falls away and the service rooms open downhill).
Moment:           a day around 1810–1820, in Jefferson's retirement.
Scenes:           outside (the house, porticoes, dome, terraces, pavilions, west lawn) and the main
                  floor cut away.
Hero view:        outside: rotation 3, from the south-west, so the west front and dome face the viewer.
                  main floor: rotation 0, from the south-east, as if entering from the east portico.
Cast:             Thomas Jefferson; his daughter Martha Jefferson Randolph and grandchildren; visitors;
                  and the enslaved people who ran the house, named where Monticello's records name
                  them (Burwell Colbert, Sally Hemings, Priscilla Hemmings, John Hemmings, Edith Fossett,
                  Wormley Hughes, Israel Gillette). Descriptions are factual and in our own words.
Life:             Jefferson writing in his cabinet, reading in the library, riding out at midday,
                  tending the greenhouse; dinner in the dining room (the timed event), served by Burwell
                  Colbert; Martha running the household; children in the parlor and tea room; visitors
                  in the entrance hall; household and outdoor work by the enslaved staff.
Scale:            outside S = 8; main floor S = 16 (people about 24 px tall at 1×).
Engine upgrades:  walls built from a rasterised floor plan (rasterPlan), so traced plans with angled
                  rooms become walls automatically; general four-cornered surfaces (patch) for hip roofs,
                  pediments and a faceted dome.
Out of scope:     cellar, second and third floors (the dome room), Mulberry Row, the vegetable garden.
```
